# Example — SaaS Beat-and-Raise (Decelerating NRR Buried in Q&A)

> **Illustrative example.** "NimbusOps Inc.", its executives, the transcript
> excerpts, and all figures below are fictional, used only to demonstrate the
> skill's output format. Not a real company and not investment advice.

## Source excerpts (illustrative)

**Prepared remarks — CEO Dana Reyes:**
> "We delivered another outstanding quarter. Q3 revenue grew 24% year over year
> to $156 million, ahead of the high end of our guidance, and non-GAAP EPS of
> $0.41 beat our outlook. Demand for our platform has never been stronger, and
> we're raising our full-year revenue guidance."

**Prepared remarks — CFO Sam Okoye:**
> "For Q4 we expect revenue of $165 to $167 million. We're raising full-year
> revenue guidance to $608–$610 million from $595–$600 million. We remain
> disciplined on profitability and now expect full-year non-GAAP operating
> margin of approximately 12%."

**Q&A:**
> **Analyst (Morgan Lane):** "Can you give us the net revenue retention number
> this quarter, and how it trended versus last quarter?"
> **CFO:** "We're really pleased with retention overall. We continue to see
> strong engagement and expansion across the base, and gross retention remains
> best-in-class. We're not going to anchor on a single quarterly figure."
> **Analyst (Morgan Lane):** "But directionally — is NRR up or down sequentially?"
> **CFO:** "There's been some modest normalization off the post-pandemic peak,
> consistent with the broader environment. We'd point you to the full-year
> framework rather than any one quarter."

## TL;DR

- **Clean beat-and-raise** on the headline: revenue +24% YoY to $156M, above the
  high end; non-GAAP EPS $0.41 beat; FY revenue guide **raised**.
- **But the key health metric — net revenue retention — was dodged twice in
  Q&A** and admitted to be "normalizing" without a number.
- Tone is **Measured**: confident and specific on the good news, evasive on the
  one metric that matters most for a usage-expansion SaaS model.
- **Watch-item:** NRR deceleration is the thing to verify next quarter — a
  raised top line can mask slowing expansion within the base.

## Guidance-change summary

- **Next quarter (Q4):** revenue $165–$167M — **Initiated** for the quarter.
- **Full year:** revenue **Raised** to $608–$610M from $595–$600M.
- **Margin:** FY non-GAAP operating margin framed up at ~12%.
- **Framing:** Guidance raise stated up front and plainly — no burying or
  euphemism on the guide itself. This is a genuine raise.

## Tone & candor score

**Measured.**

Prepared remarks are **direct and confident** on results and guidance ("ahead of
the high end", specific raise from $595–$600M to $608–$610M). The candor gap is
isolated to **one topic: net revenue retention**, where the CFO dodged twice.

Evidence quotes:
- Direct/strong: *"Q3 revenue grew 24%… ahead of the high end of our guidance."*
- Dodge #1: *"We're not going to anchor on a single quarterly figure."*
- Dodge #2 (euphemism): *"some modest normalization off the post-pandemic peak,
  consistent with the broader environment."*

Hedge/deflection read: light overall, but **clusters entirely on NRR** —
"modest", "normalization", "broader environment" are doing the work of a number
the analyst asked for twice. Q&A dodge list: **NRR (asked twice, answered
neither time with a figure).**

## Top takeaways

1. Real, clean beat-and-raise on revenue and non-GAAP EPS — top-line demand is
   genuinely strong.
2. The raise is credible and stated up front; no games on the guidance itself.
3. The one weak spot — **net revenue retention is decelerating** — was confirmed
   only by euphemism, not a number.
4. GAAP EPS was not surfaced; the GAAP/non-GAAP wedge is unquantified here and
   worth pulling from the release.

## Red flags

1. **Key metric dodged in Q&A** — NRR asked twice, answered with talking points
   and "we're not going to anchor on a single quarterly figure." For a
   usage/expansion SaaS, NRR is *the* leading indicator; refusing the number
   while the top line beats is the classic "good quarter hiding a softening
   base" pattern.
2. **Euphemized deceleration** — "modest normalization… consistent with the
   broader environment" is a soft admission NRR fell sequentially without saying
   by how much.
3. **GAAP not emphasized** — only non-GAAP EPS was highlighted; confirm the GAAP
   figure and the size of the add-backs.

## Watch-items / catalysts for next quarter

- **Get the NRR number** from the next 10-Q/supplement and track the sequential
  trend — does "modest normalization" become a slide?
- **Hold the raised FY guide** ($608–$610M) as the bar; watch whether Q4 lands in
  the $165–$167M range or the raise gets walked back.
- **Quantify the GAAP/non-GAAP wedge** — stock-based comp and any "one-time"
  add-backs behind the $0.41 non-GAAP EPS.
- **Listen for the NRR question again** next call — a second straight dodge would
  escalate this from Measured to Evasive.
